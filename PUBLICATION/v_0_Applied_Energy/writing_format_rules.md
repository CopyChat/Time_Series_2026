# Applied Energy: writing and format rules

Source: Applied Energy Guide for Authors (ScienceDirect), text supplied by the author on 2026-10-16.
Copied 2026-11-20 from HPP_potential_2026/PUBLICATION/Applied_Energy_2026; the notes specific to that paper were removed.
Elsevier artwork pages (sizing, formats) were read the same day. Each rule below is followed, where useful, by what it means for this paper.

## Files and LaTeX
- Source files must be editable (.tex for LaTeX); a PDF is not accepted as source. Double-column layout is allowed for LaTeX.
- Use the Elsevier LaTeX template (`elsarticle`; here `paper.tex` with `elsarticle-num-names.bst`). All editable source files are uploaded at submission and revision.
- No strikethrough or underline unless scientifically relevant.

## Title page
- Title concise and informative; avoid abbreviations and formulae unless widely established.
- Authors: given and family names, in the same order as in the submission system.
- Affiliations: lowercase superscript letters; full postal address including country.
- Corresponding author marked, with an email address that will be published.
- Present address, if changed: footnote with a superscript Arabic numeral.

## Abstract, keywords, highlights, graphical abstract
- **Abstract:** at most 250 words. It states the purpose, the procedures, the main findings (with statistical significance where applicable) and the principal conclusions, and emphasises what is new. It must stand alone. No references (if essential, give author and year in full); no uncommon abbreviations, or define them at first use.
- **Keywords:** 1–7, in English. Avoid multi-word keywords joined by "and" or "of"; abbreviations only if firmly established.
- **Highlights:** required, in a separate editable file with "highlights" in its name. 3–5 bullet points, each at most 85 characters including spaces, giving the new results and methods.
- **Graphical abstract:** encouraged; a separate file, at least 1328 pixels wide, landscape, readable at 13 cm width. TIFF, EPS, PDF or MS Office. Third-party material needs permission. Any generative-AI use must follow Elsevier's GenAI policy.

## Mathematics
- Equations as editable text, never images.
- Simple formulae inline; use the solidus (X/Y) for small fractions; variables in italics; powers of e written as exp.
- Display equations numbered consecutively in the order they are cited.

## Tables
- Editable text, never images; placed next to the relevant text or at the end; all cited; numbered in order of appearance; caption with each table; table notes below the body.
- **No vertical rules and no shading in cells** (booktabs style is fine).
- Use tables sparingly and do not duplicate results given elsewhere.

## Figures (artwork)
- Each figure is cited in the text, numbered in order of appearance, and supplied as a **separate file** named logically (Figure_1, Figure_2, …).
- **Caption for every figure:** a brief title (not shown on the figure itself) plus a description; little text, but every symbol and abbreviation explained.
- **Formats:**

  | Type | File type | Requirement |
  |---|---|---|
  | Vector drawings | EPS, PDF | Fonts embedded, or text saved as graphics |
  | Photographs | TIFF, JPG, PNG | At least 300 dpi (1063 px single column, 2244 px full page) |
  | Bitmapped line drawings | TIFF, JPG, PNG | At least 1000 dpi (3543 px single column, 7480 px full page) |
  | Charts and large or shaded tables | XLS, XLSX | Clearly readable |
  | Annotated images | PPT, PPTX | Legible annotations; embedded images at least 300 dpi |

- **Sizes** (Elsevier artwork sizing): single column 90 mm, 1.5 column 140 mm, full width 190 mm. Lettering about 7 pt at final size; sub- and superscripts at least 6 pt. Text must not be disproportionately small.
- **Do not combine several images or graphs in one file**, except images meant to be compared with each other (before/during/after). Multi-panel figures should therefore be panels of one comparison.
- **Colour** appears online free of charge. Colour figures must be accessible to readers with impaired colour vision (colour-blind-safe palettes, redundant marker shapes).
- **Generative AI:** may be used for explanatory diagrams, and for data visualisations derived directly from data by reproducible methods. It must not create or alter images of primary observed data. Disclose any AI use in each image caption and in the general GenAI disclosure statement.

## Supplementary material, video
- Supplementary files must be relevant, cited in the text, each with a concise caption, and submitted with the manuscript. They are published exactly as received, without typesetting. After submission they can be changed only at revision.
- Video: 150 MB per file and 1 GB in total, with a still image and a descriptive text for each file.

## Research data (Option C applies)
- **Deposit the research data in a relevant repository, and cite and link the dataset in the article**, or state why the data cannot be shared.
- A **data availability statement is required** at submission and is published with the article.
- Datasets in the reference list carry "[dataset]" before the reference: authors, title, repository, version, year and persistent identifier.
- Co-submission of a data or methods article to Data in Brief or MethodsX is possible.

## Article structure
- Numbered sections (1, 1.1, 1.1.1); cross-references use these numbers, never "the text". The abstract is not numbered.
- **Introduction:** clear objectives and adequate background; **avoid a detailed literature overview and any summary of the results**.
- **Methods:** enough detail for an independent researcher to reproduce the work. Published methods are summarised and cited; quotations go in quotation marks with a citation; modifications are described.
- **Results:** clear, concise, reproducible.
- **Discussion:** the significance of the results without repeating them; **avoid extensive citations and discussion of the literature**.
- **Conclusion:** the main conclusions, as a stand-alone section or a subsection.
- **Acknowledgements:** a separate section directly before the references (language or editing help included); nowhere else.
- **CRediT author contributions:** required.
- **Appendices:** A, B, …; equations numbered (A.1), (B.1); tables and figures Table A.1, Fig. A.1. (`elsarticle` `\appendix` does this; check that Appendix A tables are numbered A.1.)
- **Generative-AI disclosure statement:** required if AI tools were used (Elsevier GenAI policy).

## References
- Every cited item is in the list and vice versa. References in the abstract are given in full. Every reference must exist; include DOIs where available.
- Unpublished results and personal communications preferably only in the text; "in press" means accepted.
- At submission any consistent style is accepted; the journal style is applied at proof.
- **Journal style:** numbers in square brackets in order of first citation ("as shown [3,6]"; "Barnaby and Jones [8]"). In the list: the first 6 authors, then "et al."; shortened last page (51-9); journal names abbreviated according to the ISO List of Title Word Abbreviations.
- Web references: the full URL and the date last accessed, as a minimum.
- Preprints: marked "preprint" or by server name, with a DOI; replaced by the published version when one exists.

## Language
- American **or** British English, never a mix.

## Submission checklist
- A corresponding author with full contact details.
- All files uploaded (artwork, video, supplementary, tables, captions).
- Spelling and grammar checked.
- References and citations match.
- Permissions obtained for copyrighted material.
- APC responsibility understood for open access.

## Not specified in the guide
- No word or page limit, no limit on the number of figures or tables, no charge for colour online.
