Why reduce multi-decade energy time series?

Demand, wind, solar, hydro inflow, prices and flows: energy studies need many years of these series, and long records strain the models that use them.

Many years are needed

  • Same GB model, one weather year each: 41.8 GW offshore wind with 2014 data, 55.9 GW with 2012. Pfenninger 2017
  • In a 35-year record, the largest energy deficit spans 9 weeks. Ruhnau & Qvist 2022

Long records strain models

  • Europe, one node per country, 41 years at 3-hourly steps: at the limit of computational feasibility. Grochowicz et al. 2023

Part of the record is redundant

  • Diurnal and seasonal cycles repeat every year; how much can be dropped is to be measured.
  • What must be kept depends on the use.
Aim: find out how much of a long energy record is needed, at which spatial scale, and how reliable the full record itself is.
2 / 4 · Q1 · Space

From continent to island: how many periods?

How many representative periods does a spatially averaged energy series need, as the averaging area shrinks from Europe to a country to an island, and can that number be predicted from the data alone?

In plain terms: do the wind and solar series of Corsica need more representative days than those of Europe as a whole, how many more, and can the difference be predicted?

Known

  • Wind correlation between sites falls with distance; the correlation length is shorter for shorter time scales. St. Martin et al. 2015
  • Regional series are easier to aggregate than single locations. Kotzur et al. 2018

Open

  • How the number of periods needed scales with averaging area, and whether the correlation length predicts it.

Why it matters

  • A European model has been run with 1024 nodes. Frysztacki et al. 2021
  • A rule would tell island planners how much data is enough.

3 / 4 · Q2 · Time

Is the full record a reliable reference?

Every reduction is checked against the full record. Is that record long enough, and does it still describe the coming decades?

Part 1 · Record length

  • Question: how many years are needed before a statistic stops changing when more years are added?
  • Example: a mean capacity factor may settle within a few years; a 1-in-20-year wind-and-solar drought may need many decades.
  • Output: years needed per statistic and per region.

Part 2 · Climate change

  • Question: over a planning period, does the climate-driven change in a statistic grow larger than the uncertainty from Part 1?
  • Example: heating demand may change more than wind-and-solar variability.
  • Output: for each statistic, the year its change leaves the noise; until then, historical data and a fixed set of periods remain usable.

Known

  • Year-to-year persistence widened the 90 % range of annual wind capacity factors threefold (60 Canadian stations). Bodini et al. 2016
  • "Time of emergence": the year a climate signal exceeds natural variability. Hawkins & Sutton 2012

Why it matters

  • Part 1 sets the error thresholds for any reduction, including Q1.
  • Projected changes differ by variable: wind-plus-solar variability below ±5 %; heating demand −10 to −50 %. Jerez et al. 2019; Wohland et al. 2025
